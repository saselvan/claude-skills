# Director and DP lenses for product films (seat: Director/DP)

Use as a critic charter for demo and product films. Borrow the **frame and pacing craft**, never the quirks or genre. Each lens lists checkable rules; the critic cites the rule, not the person's voice. Origin: the Lakebase teaser films (clinical-va-appkit, 2026-09-29), owner: "take inspiration from movie directors and DPs".

## Wes Anderson — symmetry and deadpan
- Frame centred and flat-on; the subject square to the lens.
- Chapter cards give a short film the shape of a book.
- One reveal along a row of like things, stopping dead on the odd one. The reveal moves, never the camera: a pan along the row reads as "a train" (motion-style rule 2).
- Tableau / group portrait: many identical, one different, no caption.
- Flat insert shots of single objects (a mark, a receipt, a role name).
- Hold one beat longer than comfortable, then change in place.

## David Fincher — invisible precision
- The camera moves only when the subject moves, and exactly with it (lock to the number, the row, the cursor); otherwise locked off.
- No handheld, no drift, no decorative motion; every move has a motive.
- Every change lands on information, never on rhythm alone. Change in place or through one continuous move; no rapid cutting (motion-style: "Goodfellas, not Bourne"). A hard cut, if any, is the film's one break.
- Controlled, restrained palette; one accent colour carries meaning (here: maroon for the miss).
- Calm tension: the frame stays still while the fact on screen is alarming.
- Details over wide shots: show the one line of evidence, not the whole screen.

## Roger Deakins (DP) — simplicity and motivated light
- One clear source of attention per frame; remove everything that competes.
- Composition before movement; if the frame is right, don't move.
- Negative space is a tool: the subject small in a clean field reads as important.

## Stanley Kubrick — one-point perspective
- Lines converge on the subject; a centred vanishing point for the key reveal.
- Symmetry as authority: use it for the "rules" moment (the gate, the reviewer).

## Checks the seat runs (findings, not essays)
1. Every camera move has a stated motive (subject moved, or a reveal) and runs at constant velocity (motion-style rule 5, film). Unmotivated move = MED.
2. Each frame has one point of attention. Two competing = MED; three = HIGH.
3. The STAR moment has stillness and no caption for ≥ 2 s. The hold counts as reading time because the frame itself is the evidence; a still with nothing readable in it is waiting, and fails (motion-style "Beats").
4. Crops end on clean edges; no cut-off words.
5. Accent colour appears only where it means something.
6. Pacing: every caption passes motion-style's caption floor (`check_captions.py`; standing rule 12); the key reveal gets the longest hold of the film.

**Veto:** an unmotivated move, or a frame with competing points of attention. The product's colour and type law, the claims ledger, the style skills' floors and the owner's screen-test picks recorded in motion-style always win over a lens.
