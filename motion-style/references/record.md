# Motion screen-test record

The record behind the motion-style skill. Each test showed the same content animated two or more ways that differed in one respect. Quotations are the user's words, lightly corrected for typos. Content in the tests was illustrative, and every number shown was a placeholder.

Read this when a rule is ambiguous or when revising the skill.

## Pacing and sequence

M1. Pacing: four lines drawing from one box, fast and staggered versus slow and staggered.
"In this instance the order means nothing and adds no value. I would take slower, but not the one after the other."

M1b. Duration, all lines at once: 0.45, 0.75, and 1.1 seconds.
Picked 0.75 s. "But the words appearing a beat after the lines appears off to my brain."

M1c. Labels: present from the start, fading in with the lines, or appearing as each line arrives.
Picked fading in with the lines. "It barely looks different from A at this speed, but B for sure."

M2. Cause and effect: four steps at once versus in sequence.
Picked the sequence. "But again, the way the words appear is hurting my brain."

M2b. Text treatments (slow fade, instant, quick fade).
"None."

M2c. What moves (words static, dim-then-brighten, draw-only).
The user diagnosed: "The transitions are not smooth. Look up Emil's skills or rules."

M2d. The same sequence rebuilt to Emil Kowalski's rules.
"In movie language, whatever you showed me feels like Bourne cuts, not Goodfellas."

M3. The long take: tracking shot, one sweeping reveal, or a gliding focus.
One-shot playback: the sweep. "B feels so much better to my eyes and brain." Looping playback: the gliding focus. "I'd be like, I already know what is coming, why be coy?" The tracking shot: "A feels like a train and very off."

M4. The loop seam: snap back, dim together, or a continuous beam.
"Definitely not C." Asked for expert input; after the research (abrupt onsets capture attention, gradual changes don't), recorded B, dim together.

M5. Emphasis: color fill, scale, dimming the rest, or a ring.
Picked the color fill. "Because I am the guy who never knows what his remote has selected in Apple TV."

M6. In-app panel speed: 160, 250, and 400 ms.
"B or C, I am okay with either."

M7. Velocity: constant versus eased.
"Yes, that is it. I was wondering why I liked both." Constant for film, eased for explainers and apps; the mapping was Claude's proposal and the user kept it.

## Palette

Round 1: brass on near-black, saturated gold on near-black, teal on near-black.
"I do not know why we have the teal, TBH. The black and gold reads as try too hard to be classy."
"Teal works in certain contexts but not with this black."

Round 2, from the visual record: Rothko oxblood, winter pale, Banff glacial.
"I love A, but I am worried about readability and accessibility... projectors or washed-out LCD TVs in large rooms." "I like winter, but would appreciate more depth in the background." "I am more of a forest green... the background at the very back is working for me, but not the highlight color."

Round 3, with a projector wash simulation: Rothko with lifted text, winter with deeper shadows, forest with vermilion, forest with saffron.
"I think B2 wins." Of B2: "Looks nothing like impressionist snow, by the way. Absolutely flat."

Round 4: B2 flat versus B3 broken color with grain.
"B2 for sure. I love B3, but it will confuse the fuck out of people. This looks like a washed-out disco room."

Direction: "Let us stick as close to the house style as possible. I spent a lot of time on it." And: "Don't go by the film prompt. Stick to house style and our screen tests."

## Scenarios

S1. A number changing: counting through, crossfade, roll.
"B, or C if it was slower. Definitely not A."

S1b. Roll speed: crossfade at 0.75 s, roll at 1.1 s, roll at 1.4 s.
"Yeah, C2, but it depends on the context. C1 could work too." In-app value changes were later set to 400 to 500 ms on expert guidance.

S2. Before and after: whole-frame crossfade, only what changes, sweep.
Of the sweep: "The text is overlaid, so it is out immediately." Picked only what changes.

S2b. The sweep with the caption pulled out of it.
"C2 feels a little jarring, but they feel identical to me."

S3. Captions changing: crossfade, roll, dip.
First shown side by side: "It is so confusing seeing them one below the other. My eyes are confused." With a switcher: "B. The words appear too abruptly. Not the transition, but they just pop."

S3b. Roll variants: as before, late opacity, slowed to 1.1 s.
"Slow works, could be slower, LOL."

S4. A chart updating: in place at 0.75 s, in place at 1.4 s, collapse and regrow.
"I see no difference between A and B." Of collapse and regrow: "About the same. I will defer to the experts here." Recorded in-place change, per Heer and Robertson.

S5. A list arriving: all at once, 50 ms stagger, 150 ms stagger.
"C maybe, no great preference." Later set to about 50 ms on expert guidance, since staggering helps only to signal rank.

S6. Overview to detail: camera zoom, the step expands, fill then crossfade.
"B. I would like C if that final shot weren't so abrupt and jarring."

S6b. The step expands versus fill, hold, and slow crossfade.
"Yes, both actually."

S7. A walk through a graph: trace one path, rings step by step, one sweep.
"B and C make no sense in a story telling point of view, what is the story?" Asked whether every walk must open on a question and end on an answer: "Too prescriptive."

S8. Scroll: scrubbed, triggered, pinned steps.
"Definitely not C. I think B works when it is vertical with a vertical scroll. Vertical scroll with a horizontal motion is not doing it for me. More like Atavist works with progressive disclosure rather than one animation."

S8b and S8c. Vertical: one sweep versus progressive disclosure.
"Progressive, but only when the article is long and the sections therein make sense."

S9. Press and hover: color only, color and press, plus lift.
"B or C, either one works."

S10. Waiting: spinner, shimmer, pulse, real steps.
"D wherever possible."
