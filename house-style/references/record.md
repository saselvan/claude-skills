## Laddering

Each rung answers "why does that matter?" or "what do you see?" about the rung above.

1. Hasselblad
   Thread A: "The solidity." → "Means I can hold it and it will feel good." → "Tactility and absence of tackiness."
   Tacky means: "Trying too hard to be cool, unnecessary adornments, look-at-me design that screams with no utility."
   Thread B: "Sheer faith that this is a serious piece of work." → "I am going to be using it and spending time with it, so I want it to be consistent and reliable."

5. Jeffrey Gibson
   "The colors and the in-your-faceness." → "Because in a world where beige is considered respectable, here he is showing a verve for life." → "I don't know, why shouldn't it? Because it is exciting and inviting."

6. Mickalene Thomas
   "Combining media I wouldn't have thought possible." → "It shows creativity and making unrelated parts a holistic, meaningful whole." → "It is literally my job to combine multiple moving pieces to make a useful whole and somehow get the message across that it is useful, worth it, and in itself an object and act of beauty." → Asked why it must be beautiful if already useful: "Because what fun is a wholly utilitarian world."

7. Jeanne Kefer by Knopff
   "The composition and colors." → "Everything breathes." → "No elements fighting, everything in its place." → "Because it gets me calm and I pay attention to everything."

8. Rothko
   "Depths." → Chosen from Claude's options, all three: soft, feathered edges that make the rectangles seem to hover; thin layered washes that make the color seem lit from behind; scale that fills your field of vision up close. → "It does not matter, I just love it."

9. Mondrian
   "Colors." → "I do not know, it just makes me happy." → "Maybe, I would have to see them, but I do not see pastels working in the sharp lines he draws." → Shown the same grid in pure colors and in pastels, would you still love the pastel one? "No." → Shown the grid without its black lines: "No, because the gray or paper background just killed it and it is just random looking."

## Triads

Which two belong together, and why is the third different?

1. Mickalene Thomas, Amazon.com, Jeanne Kefer by Knopff
   Together: Mickalene Thomas and Jeanne Kefer. Odd one out: Amazon.com, "ugly, cluttered."
   Why Mickalene Thomas, who has a lot going on, isn't cluttered: "Because there's a vision behind it and the disparate items flow together and it is beautiful."

2. Databricks workspace, Teenage Engineering, Leica Q
   Together: Teenage Engineering and Leica Q. Odd one out: Databricks workspace.
   Leica's restraint versus the workspace's lack of color: "The workspace is something I am looking at actively. Leica is not something I am looking at actively; it is the rangefinder I am looking through."

3. Modern interior design, Rothko, impressionist winter paintings
   Together: Rothko and impressionist winter paintings. Odd one out: modern interior design.
   Winter whites versus beige: "Their pales are not uniform, they show shades of light, and are not a duplication of reality but evocation."

4. NYT infographics, PowerBI, Mondrian
   Together: NYT infographics and Mondrian. Odd one out: PowerBI.
   His grid versus theirs: "The grids are uglier, don't space well, and most of them have no fucking color."
