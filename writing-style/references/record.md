# Writing interview record

The record behind the writing-style skill. Quotations are the user's words, lightly corrected for typos. Where the user chose from options Claude offered, the record says so. A few personal or self-critical remarks have been left out or stored neutrally at the user's direction; the omissions are marked.

Read this when a rule is ambiguous, when judging a borderline case, or when revising the skill.

Contents: loves (1–18), dislikes (D1–D5), the user's own work (O1–O6), stated preferences, rewrite tests (1–21).

## Rewrite tests

Each test showed the same content written two ways that differed in one respect. The content was illustrative.

1. Opening. A: argument first. B: scene first.
   Picked A. "But they keep telling me it should be B."
2. Sentence rhythm. A: short sentences. B: longer sentences.
   Picked A. "But can't read as too abrupt when read aloud, so the brain doesn't feel harried."
3. Register. A: plain. B: elevated and moral.
   First said "Hybrid." Of a hybrid with B's moral line: "Kinda sounds pandering and LinkedIn thought-leadery." Final pick A: "This personalizing feels so unearned to me."
4. Wit. A: straight. B: one dry line at the end.
   Picked A. "Only because when I saw vendors use B, I saw at least one customer think 'what a load of crock,' and that customer turned out to be the one I resonated with the most." (personal remainder omitted)
5. Naming the pattern. A: described. B: named ("governance drift").
   "Both actually," then "B most times, because it uses fewer words."
6. Certainty. A: flat ("breaks," "every team"). B: qualified, with "in the setups I've seen."
   In conversation: B, "literally how I speak to customers." In text: A, "only because it's not like outside of these calls I am some well-quoted expert."
6b. Certainty without the "I." A: flat. C: qualified, no personal anchor.
   Picked C. "Every is a lie technically, because I am not omniscient."
7. First person. A: impersonal. B: "I've watched teams..."
   "Could be either really." B "drifts into sharing territory rather than observing territory. Not a memoirist, more of a PD James."
8. Endings. A: statement. B: question to the reader.
   Slides: B. Personal blog: A, "because I am not asking for them to reach out to me." Databricks blog: "More prescriptive, CTA at the end."
9. Lists. A: prose. B: bulleted.
   Picked A. "No list. Screams AI."
10. Asides. A: none. B: one parenthetical.
    Picked A.
11. Analogy. A: literal. B: one analogy.
    Blog: A. Slide speaker notes: B.
12. Acronyms for the public. A: assumed. B: explained.
    Picked B. "Always good to expand on acronyms."
12b. Acronyms for an internal team. A: unexpanded. B: expanded.
    Picked A. "They all know DBUs."
13. Titles. A: descriptive. B: crafted.
    Picked B.
13b. A crafted title alone, or with a plain subtitle.
    Picked the title with a subtitle.
14. Contrast density. A: leans on "X, not Y." B: same points without it.
    Picked B. The user identified the pattern as a crutch they once loved; their reasons are stored neutrally: internal writing must stay measured, and the author's frustration should never show.
15. Em dashes. A: with. B: commas and periods.
    Picked B. "When I wrote papers in grad school, I loved em dashes."
16. Concreteness. A: general. B: concrete numbers.
    Picked B.
17. LinkedIn line breaks. A: paragraphs. B: one sentence per line.
    "A never B."
18. The other side. A: own position only. B: strongest opposing case, then the answer.
    Picked B, "but less verbose if it is possible. Making the reader do too much work." A version tightened from 58 to 38 words: "Better."
19. Labeling opinion. A: stated as analysis. B: "My read:".
    Picked A. "Never stuff like 'my read.' I just don't feel comfortable with that voice unless it is experiments I have done and proved for a fact." (personal remainder omitted)
20. Addressing the reader. A: about teams. B: "you."
    Blogs and posts: A. Slides, for a team the user has met a few times: B.
21. LinkedIn post length. A: about 60 words. B: about 150 words.
    Picked A.

After the rules were approved, the user added the baseline: "Never go with unearned familiarity, oversharing, and feigned personableness. That is the one tangible baseline I would go for, so I am not cringing when I read it tomorrow."
